How the numbers are built

Goals — Dixon-Coles bivariate Poisson. The low-score correction matters: independent Poisson misprices 0-0, 1-0 and 1-1, which is where under 2.5 and BTTS value hides.

Corners, cards, shots — negative binomial, not Poisson. Real match counts are over-dispersed (variance exceeds the mean), and using Poisson would systematically under-price the overs.

Both teams X+ corners / bookings — priced from a genuine joint distribution. Both sides share a latent game-state factor (an open, stretched game; a strict referee), so the two teams' counts are correlated. Multiplying two marginals under-prices these lines, and that is the usual mistake.

Player props — per-90 rates scaled by a mixture over minutes scenarios rather than a point estimate, so a rotation risk shows up as real mass at zero. Fouls are committed, not suffered. Bookings are derived from the foul distribution through a per-foul card hazard, scaled by referee strictness.

Fair prices — the book's margin is removed before any comparison, using the power method on wide markets and proportional on tight ones. Skipping this step makes almost everything look like value.

Parlays — cross-match legs are independent, so multiplying is correct. Same-game legs are not: they run through a Gaussian copula. A slip is scored on a correlation-adjusted probability, never on a naive product.

Player layer — every player is mapped to a functional role (inverted winger, holding midfielder, aggressive stopper…), which supplies the twenty-odd per-90 metrics not known for them individually. Shots on target are built as a chain — expected shots × on-target rate — rather than as an independent rate, and goals from shots on target × conversion.

Team ↔ player consistency — the two directions are reconciled. Top-down, the team's expected shots are distributed by involvement share. Bottom-up, individual booking probabilities are summed into a team card total and blended with the team rating. A board where the strikers' shot props exceed the team's shot total is quietly incoherent; the reconciliation factor is reported so a large disagreement is visible rather than hidden.

Matchups — a full-back's foul rate is not a property of the full-back alone. Wide attackers are paired against the opposing defender, and dribble volume and pace gap shade both his fouls and the attacker's fouls won. The duel is resolved in one direction and then the other, so a foul counts once from each end.
